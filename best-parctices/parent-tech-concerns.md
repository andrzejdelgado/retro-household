# Parent Tech Concerns

What parents of children aged 0 to 8 worry about regarding social media, mobile phones, AI and modern technology, combining US and European research. Where US and European findings overlap, they are merged into one concern with the regional differences noted inside it.

**Caveat on the data.** Most large surveys cover children 9 and up. The 0 to 8 evidence comes from Common Sense Media and Pew in the US, Ofcom in the UK, and health agencies in Sweden, France and Denmark. Where a figure is regional, it is labelled.

## 1. Screens displacing what builds a young brain

The concern the child development community treats as most serious for this age. Time on devices replaces face-to-face interaction, free play, movement, sleep and reading. Parents name language delay, short attention spans, weak self-regulation and poorer motor skills. Daily reading among US 5 to 8 year olds fell from 64% to 52% since 2017. 74% of UK parents of 3 to 5 year olds worry about excessive screen time. In Europe this is framed as public health: Sweden advises zero screens under 2 and one hour a day to age 5, and France prints its no-screens-under-3 advice in every child's health booklet.

## 2. Addiction, meltdowns, and manipulative design

Devices become the only thing that calms the child, removal triggers tantrums, and the child never learns to tolerate boredom. Gaming time among US 0 to 8 year olds rose 65% since 2020, and 40% have a tablet by age 2. European parents place the blame more on the product than the child: autoplay, infinite scroll and reward loops engineered to hold a 4 year old. The proposed EU Kids Act would ban such design for minors, which shows how mainstream that view has become.

## 3. Harmful or inappropriate content reaching the child

The top-ranked fear in most surveys on both continents. Violent, sexual or frightening material arriving through algorithmic feeds, mainly YouTube and short video, plus misinformation the child cannot judge. 68% of UK parents of 3 to 5 year olds and 72% of EU parents cite it. Supervision is uneven: 62% of US parents sometimes co-watch YouTube, only 17% co-watch TikTok. In the UK, 37% of 3 to 5 year olds already used a social or video app in 2024.

## 4. Privacy, data, and stranger contact

Roughly half of US parents rank privacy and safety first. Worries include apps and toys recording voice and behaviour, the child's digital footprint before they can consent, and grooming through chat in games, cited by 61% of EU parents. European parents, shaped by GDPR, are more likely to see tracking as a rights violation rather than an annoyance.

## 5. AI: false answers, fake friends, and lost thinking

The newest and fastest-growing concern everywhere. Nearly three in four US parents of 0 to 8 year olds fear AI toys or assistants will say something inappropriate, untrue or unsafe. About 70% fear loss of critical thinking if AI does the effortful work. Parents on both continents worry about children bonding with chatbots instead of people and being unable to tell real from synthetic content. Europe has moved to legislate: the Kids Act would bar under-13s from AI companions, keep them off by default for minors, and prohibit designs that simulate relationships to create dependency.

## 6. Mental health and social skills downstream

Social media is rare under 8, but parents are already anxious. 83% of US parents believe children's mental health is declining and 36% plan to never allow social media. One in three EU adolescents report feeling stressed or excluded because of social media, and children who started before 10 later spend far more time on screens. Parents of young children read this as a preview and want to delay the starting age as long as possible.

## 7. The collective action problem

Parents feel that limits are pointless if their child's peers have none. This is where the continents diverge most. American parents mostly manage it individually and feel outmatched. European parents expect the state and each other to act: 92% of Europeans call online child protection a top priority, parents are the strongest supporters of age bans at 79% in Britain, Italy and Spain, France and Denmark have passed under-15 laws, Denmark banned screens in daycare for under-2s, and two thirds of Dutch primary schools run parent pacts to delay smartphones together.

## 8. Their own behaviour

Parents feel guilt about their own phone use in front of their kids and about using a screen as a babysitter when they lack alternatives. In Germany 54% of parents prefer family activities that involve screens, highest among full-time workers. Swedish and Dutch interviews show this weighs most on lower-income households.
